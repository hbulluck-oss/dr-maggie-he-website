## Understanding your blood pressure reading

When your blood pressure is measured, you get two numbers — for example, 130/82 mmHg. But what do these numbers actually mean, and when should you be concerned?

## The two numbers explained

### Systolic pressure (the top number)

This is the pressure in your arteries when your heart beats and pushes blood out. It is the higher of the two numbers.

### Diastolic pressure (the bottom number)

This is the pressure in your arteries between heartbeats, when your heart is relaxing and filling with blood. It is the lower number.

## What is a normal blood pressure?

| Category | Systolic | Diastolic |
|----------|----------|-----------|
| **Normal** | Below 120 | Below 80 |
| **Elevated** | 120 to 129 | Below 80 |
| **High (Stage 1)** | 130 to 139 | 80 to 89 |
| **High (Stage 2)** | 140 or above | 90 or above |

A single high reading does not necessarily mean you have high blood pressure. Blood pressure varies throughout the day and can be affected by stress, caffeine, exercise, and even the anxiety of being in a clinic (known as "white coat hypertension").

## Why does high blood pressure matter?

High blood pressure is sometimes called the "silent killer" because it usually causes no symptoms. Over time, it damages the walls of your arteries and increases your risk of:

- **Heart attack**
- **Stroke**
- **Heart failure**
- **Kidney disease**
- **Vascular dementia**

## How is it diagnosed properly?

Because blood pressure can fluctuate, your doctor may suggest:

- **Ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM)** — wearing a cuff for 24 hours that takes regular readings during your normal daily activities
- **Home blood pressure monitoring** — using a validated home device to take readings over several days

These give a much more accurate picture than a single clinic reading.

## What can you do about it?

### Lifestyle changes (effective for everyone)

- **Reduce salt intake** — aim for less than 6 grams per day
- **Regular exercise** — at least 150 minutes of moderate activity per week
- **Maintain a healthy weight** — even a small amount of weight loss can make a meaningful difference
- **Limit alcohol** — stick within recommended limits
- **Eat more fruit, vegetables, and whole grains**

### Medication

If lifestyle changes are not enough, or if your blood pressure is significantly raised, your doctor may recommend medication. There are several types, and the choice depends on your age, background, and other health conditions.

## The bottom line

Knowing your numbers is the first step. If your blood pressure is consistently above 130/80, speak to your doctor. High blood pressure is very treatable, and getting it under control is one of the most important things you can do for your long-term heart health.
