## Not all chest pain is a heart attack

Chest pain is one of the most common reasons people visit their doctor or attend the Emergency Department. While it can be frightening, it is important to know that many causes of chest pain are not related to the heart at all.

That said, chest pain should always be taken seriously until a clear cause has been found.

## Common causes of chest pain

### Heart-related causes

- **Angina** — a tight or heavy feeling in the chest caused by reduced blood flow to the heart muscle, usually during exertion
- **Heart attack** — a sudden blockage of a coronary artery, causing severe and prolonged chest pain
- **Pericarditis** — inflammation of the lining around the heart, often causing a sharp pain that worsens when lying flat

### Non-heart causes

- **Musculoskeletal pain** — strain of the chest wall muscles or ribs, often made worse by movement or pressing on the area
- **Acid reflux (GORD)** — a burning sensation behind the breastbone, often after eating or when lying down
- **Anxiety** — can cause chest tightness, palpitations, and a feeling of breathlessness
- **Lung conditions** — such as a chest infection, pleurisy, or, rarely, a pulmonary embolism

## When should you worry?

**Call 000 immediately if you experience:**

- Sudden, severe chest pain lasting more than 15 minutes
- Chest pain with sweating, nausea, or feeling faint
- Chest pain spreading to your left arm, neck, jaw, or back
- Severe breathlessness with chest pain

These symptoms could indicate a heart attack, and rapid treatment saves lives.

## When to see your GP or a cardiologist

If your chest pain is not an emergency but keeps coming back, especially with exertion, it is worth getting it checked. Your doctor may arrange:

- An **electrocardiogram (ECG)** to check your heart rhythm
- **Blood tests** to look for signs of heart damage
- An **exercise stress test** or **CT coronary angiogram** to check the arteries supplying your heart

## The bottom line

Most chest pain turns out not to be a heart attack. But you should never ignore it. If in doubt, get it checked — and if it is severe or sudden, call 000 without delay.
