## What is a coronary angiogram?

A coronary angiogram is a test that uses X-rays and a special dye to look at the arteries supplying blood to your heart. It is one of the most important tests in cardiology and helps your doctor see whether there are any narrowings or blockages that could be causing your symptoms.

## Why might you need one?

Your cardiologist may recommend a coronary angiogram if:

- You have chest pain (angina) that has not been fully explained by other tests
- A CT scan or stress test has suggested a possible narrowing in a heart artery
- You have had a heart attack and the blocked artery needs to be identified
- You have heart failure and the cause needs to be investigated

## Before the procedure

- You will usually be asked not to eat for a few hours beforehand
- You can normally take your regular medications with a sip of water (check with your team)
- Blood tests will be done to check your kidney function and blood clotting
- The team will explain the procedure and ask you to sign a consent form
- You will change into a hospital gown and have a small cannula placed in your hand or arm

## During the procedure

1. **You will be awake** throughout. A local anaesthetic numbs the area where the catheter is inserted — usually the wrist (radial artery) or, less commonly, the groin (femoral artery).

2. **A thin, flexible tube (catheter)** is guided through the artery up to your heart. You should not feel this.

3. **A contrast dye** is injected through the catheter. This shows up on the X-ray screen, allowing your cardiologist to see the coronary arteries in detail. You may feel a brief warm, flushing sensation when the dye is injected.

4. **X-ray images** are taken from different angles. The whole process usually takes 15 to 30 minutes.

5. **If a significant narrowing is found**, your cardiologist may discuss treating it there and then with angioplasty and stenting — or may recommend a separate procedure depending on the complexity.

## After the procedure

- The catheter is removed and pressure is applied to the wrist or groin to stop any bleeding
- You will rest on the ward for a few hours — usually 2 to 4 hours if the wrist was used
- Most patients go home the same day
- You should avoid heavy lifting or strenuous activity for a few days
- Your cardiologist will explain the findings and discuss the next steps with you

## Is it safe?

A coronary angiogram is a very common and generally safe procedure. Serious complications are rare (less than 1 in 1,000). Minor bruising at the wrist or groin is the most common side effect.

## The bottom line

A coronary angiogram gives your cardiologist the clearest possible picture of your heart arteries. It is a short procedure, you are awake throughout, and most people go home the same day.
